Everest Base Camp Trek — Essentials

Route and Duration
The classic EBC trek runs Lukla to Everest Base Camp (5,364m) and back,
typically 12-14 days including acclimatization days. The trek does not
require technical climbing skills, but sustained daily walking at
altitude is demanding.

Getting to the Trailhead
Most trekkers fly from Kathmandu to Lukla (Tenzing-Hillary Airport,
2,860m), a short but weather-dependent flight often delayed or
rerouted through Manthali during peak season. An alternative is
driving to Salleri and walking an extra 3-4 days to reach Lukla,
avoiding the flight entirely.

Acclimatization
Two rest days are standard: one at Namche Bazaar (3,440m) and one at
Dingboche (4,410m) or Pheriche. The rule of thumb above 3,000m is to
not increase sleeping altitude by more than 300-500m per day. Symptoms
of acute mountain sickness (headache, nausea, dizziness) should not be
ignored; descent is the primary treatment.

Costs
A guided teahouse trek typically runs $1,200-2,000 USD per person for
12-14 days, covering permits, guide, porter, and basic lodging (meals
usually extra, roughly $25-35/day as prices rise with altitude).
Independent trekking without a guide is legally required to have at
least a permit-registered guide as of recent regulation changes.

Required Permits
- Sagarmatha National Park entry permit
- Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality entry permit
Both are checked at Monjo village, shortly after Lukla.

Best Season
Late September to November (post-monsoon, clear skies) and March to
May (pre-monsoon, warmer, rhododendrons in bloom) are the two trekking
windows. Winter (Dec-Feb) is possible but very cold at higher camps;
monsoon (Jun-Aug) brings poor visibility and leech-heavy lower trails.

Connectivity
NCell and Ntc SIM cards work in lower villages; Everest Link wifi
cards are sold at most teahouses above Namche for a fee per device.
